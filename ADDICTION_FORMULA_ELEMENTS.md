# The Addiction Formula: Six Elements of Songwriting

Based on principles from "The Addiction Formula" by Friedemann Findeisen, this guide covers the six elements that work together to create energy and engagement in songs.

## The Six Elements (Ranked by Power)

### 1. **Arrangement** (Most Powerful)

The texture and instrumentation of a section - which instruments play, when they enter/exit, and how full the sound is.

**How it controls HYPE:**

- Fuller texture (more frequency coverage) = Higher hype
- Sparser texture = Lower hype
- Add/remove instruments every 4-8 bars for directional energy

**Texture Spectrum (Low to High Hype):**

- None (silence)
- Highs only (treble)
- Mids only (midrange)
- Lows only (bass)
- Mids + Highs
- Lows + Mids
- Lows + Highs (sparse middle = interesting texture)
- Full (all three ranges)

**How it creates TENSION:**

- **Regular Tension:** Add or remove instruments mid-section to create change within a moment
- **Implied Tension:** Noticeably strip out elements to create a "missing piece" that pulls toward the next section (e.g., remove bass to signal a bass drop coming)

**Critical Principle - Implied Tension:**
This is one of the most sophisticated and underused techniques. When done right, it allows you to maintain engagement while dropping hype. For example, after a high-energy chorus, instead of just dropping everything, you might remove the kick drum and bass while keeping vocals and pads - listeners feel something is missing and anticipate the refill.

---

### 2. **Rhythm** (Strongest for Tension)

Beat patterns, note subdivisions, and rhythmic density.

**Note Subdivisions (Low to High Hype):**

- Whole notes (sub1) - minimal
- Half notes (sub2)
- Quarter notes (sub4) - standard
- Eighth notes (sub8) - common in verses and choruses
- Sixteenth notes (sub16) - highest, used in pre-chorus builds and climaxes

**How it controls HYPE:**

- Higher subdivisions = higher hype
- More instruments playing the highest subdivision = higher hype
- Legato (smooth, connected) = higher hype than staccato (short, separated)
- Shorter phrase length = higher hype than longer phrases

**How it creates TENSION:**

- **Subdivision Jumps:** Change from sub8 (verses) to sub16 (pre-chorus) mid-section for immediate energy boost
- **Broken Flow:** Hint at a higher subdivision without fully delivering it (implied tension) - audience hears fragments of a faster rhythm but not the full pattern
- **Contrary Movement:** Place drums off-beat for unexpected, unsettling rhythm (implied tension)

**Rhythm Power:**
Rhythm is the most useful element for creating tension. Many hit songs use rhythm to drive the energy curve where other elements might fail.

---

### 3. **Harmony** (Directional Pull)

Chord progressions and harmonic movement.

**Harmonic Tension by Starting Chord:**

- **Tonic Chords** (I, vi, iii in major; i, VI, III in minor) - **LOW tension**
- **Subdominant Chords** (IV, ii, II in major; iv, IV in minor) - **MEDIUM tension**
- **Dominant Chords** (V, bVII in major; V, VII, bII, v in minor) - **HIGH tension**

A section starting on a dominant chord naturally feels more tense than one starting on tonic. The 12-bar blues (I-I-I-I / IV-IV-I-I / V-IV-I-V) perfectly demonstrates this three-way progression.

**How it controls HYPE:**

- Wider voicings = higher hype (more frequency coverage)
- Closer voicings = lower hype
- **Key changes** are the most dramatic hype increase possible (use very sparingly - maybe once per album)

**How it creates TENSION:**

- Build tension at the END of progressions using "home and away" concept
  - Home = I (major) or vi (minor) = safe, resolved
  - Away = everything else = varying degrees of tension
  - Farther away from home = more tension
- Use suspended chords (Vsus before V) for mid-progression tension
- Descending bass lines can build tension gradually

**How it creates IMPLIED TENSION:**

- Use inversions (rootless voicings) or power chords (no third) to create absence of familiar sounds

---

### 4. **Part-Writing** (Individual Instrument Lines)

What each instrument plays - dynamics, phrasing, ornamentation.

**Dynamics (Loudness):**

- Most connected to overall energy of any element
- Doubling volume approximately quadruples energy (wave energy ∝ amplitude²)
- Choruses typically 2-3x louder than verses

**For Vocals Specifically:**

**Vocal Modes (Low to High Hype):**

1. **Speech** - Conversational, rap-like (low-medium hype)
2. **Neutral** - Natural singing voice (medium hype)
3. **Falsetto** - Light, airy quality (medium-high hype)
4. **Belting** - Powerful, projected, emotional (highest hype)

**Vocal Effects:**

- **Twang** - Adds nasal/oral resonance, raises hype (especially in choruses)
- **Vibrato** - Adds rhythmic oscillation, increases hype and interest
- **Ornamentations** - Fast melodic runs (RnB style), increase complexity and hype
- **Distortion** - Adds edge/aggression
  - Growling (low pitch) ← → Screaming (high pitch)

**How Vocals Control HYPE:**

- **Range:** Higher pitch in vocals = higher hype (chorus has highest notes, verse has lowest)
- **Variety:** More different notes = higher hype (one-note melody = lowest)
- Shift between vocal modes mid-section (Neutral → Belting) for tension
- Gradually increase singing power/intensity for tension buildup

**How Vocals Create IMPLIED TENSION:**

- **Subtract the melody:** Remove vocals right before chorus (powerful technique)
- **One-note tension:** Use a single note that's NOT in your home chord (creates friction with harmony)
- **Space/breathing:** Leave gaps between vocal phrases instead of continuous singing
- **Attitude contrast:** Low-energy vocal delivery over high-energy music (or vice versa) for emotional impact

**Critical Principle - The Human Voice:**
All successful instruments in pop music share one thing: closeness to the human voice. Guitar wah-pedals mimic speech ("wah wah"), dubstep bass mimics angry "WHAT", saxophones are closest to vocal timbre. This is why vocals in every section keep engagement highest.

## The Communication Model

Think of songwriting as a communication chain:

```
YOUR SIDE:                    THEIR SIDE:
Thought (energy curve)   →    Decryption (perception)
     ↓                              ↓
Encryption (6 elements)  ←    Feeling (like/dislike/engage)
```

**Success = Listener can draw your energy curve after listening**

If they draw a different or flatter curve than you intended, your encryption failed. Control your side well (thought + encryption), and their side takes care of itself.

## Key Principles

### 1. Elements Work Together

No single element determines your energy curve. The interplay of multiple elements creates the final result. For example:

- One element goes DOWN in hype
- But another element goes UP in hype
- Overall energy can STAY UP or UP despite the first element

### 2. Relative Values

Energy is entirely relative. You cannot determine a section's energy level without hearing what comes before and after. The graph scales to fit each song's extremes.

### 3. Power Ranking

Not all techniques are equally powerful:

- Arrangement is most powerful
- Rhythm is most useful for tension
- Lyrics are least powerful for energy shaping
- Production is important but subtle

### 4. Power Scaling

- 2x volume ≈ 4x energy (amplitude squared relationship)
- Subdivision jumps create immediate energy shift
- Harmonic tension builds gradually

## Practical Application Strategy

### For Higher Hype

1. ✓ Add instruments to arrangement (most obvious)
2. ✓ Increase rhythmic subdivision (more notes)
3. ✓ Raise vocal pitch
4. ✓ Add vocal variety (more melodic notes)
5. ✓ Increase loudness/dynamics
6. ✓ Shift to higher vocal mode (Speech → Neutral → Belting)
7. ✓ Add key change (most dramatic, very rare)

### For Creating Tension

1. ✓ **Rhythm changes** - Jump subdivisions, broken flow patterns
2. ✓ **Harmony movement** - Build chord progressions toward dominant
3. ✓ **Arrangement additions** - Layer instruments mid-section
4. ✓ **Crescendos** - Gradually increase volume
5. ✓ **Melodic ascent** - Raise vocal pitch gradually
6. ✓ **Vocal mode shifts** - Move to more powerful mode
7. ✓ **Effect addition** - Gradually add twang/distortion

### For IMPLIED Tension (Most Sophisticated)

1. ✓ **Noticeably strip arrangement** - Remove expected instrument (bass, kick, snare, hi-hat)
2. ✓ **Broken rhythms** - Hint at higher subdivision without delivering
3. ✓ **Vocal gaps** - Use space/rests in melody
4. ✓ **Missing note** - One-note melody on non-home chord
5. ✓ **Attitude contrast** - Emotional vocal over energetic music (or vice versa)

**Key: Must be NOTICEABLE. If it's not noticeable, you're just dropping energy normally.**

## Examples of Elements in Hit Songs

**"Wrecking Ball" - Miley Cyrus**

- Arrangement: Sparse intro, builds gradually
- Vocals: High belted notes in chorus, lower chest voice in verse
- Harmony: Key changes for dramatic effect
- Rhythm: Legato, longer phrases for emotional space

**"Blank Space" - Taylor Swift**

- Arrangement: IMPLIED TENSION - sparse arrangement with noticeable absences
- Rhythm: Sub8 verse but vocals/drums hint at sub16 (broken flow)
- Vocals: Attitude contrast - conversational/speech delivery
- Implied Tension: Heavy use throughout

**"Chandelier" - Sia**

- Arrangement: Builds through layers
- Vocals: Belting in chorus, varied modes throughout
- Rhythm: Subdivision jumps for energy
- Effects: Vocal layering and effects

## Reference: The 6-Element Framework

| Element | Rank | Primary Use | Technique |
| --------- | ------ | ------------ | ----------- |
| Arrangement | 1 - Most Powerful | Setting hype directly | Add/remove instruments every 4-8 bars |
| Rhythm | 2 - Tension Master | Creating tension | Subdivision jumps, broken flow patterns |
| Harmony | 3 - Directional | Building anticipation | Start on dominant chords, move toward resolution |
| Part-Writing | 4 - Detail & Nuance | Vocal control, dynamics | Mode shifts, range changes, effects |
| Production | 5 - Subtle Enhancement | Coloring, polish | Effects, filters, mixing tricks |
| Lyrics | 6 - Least Powerful | Meaning, storytelling | Emotional delivery matches energy |

## Study Method

To master these elements:

1. **Listen actively** - Pick a song and identify which elements create each energy shift
2. **Draw energy curves** - Have a friend listen and draw your song's curve without seeing yours - did they capture your intent?
3. **Isolate elements** - Listen to just drums, then just harmony, then just vocals - notice which creates the most impact
4. **Learn by example** - Compare high-energy sections to low-energy sections in favorite songs
5. **Experiment** - Try each technique in isolation, then combined with others

---

## Remember

- **When in doubt, always go UP** (in hype, tension, or energy)
- **Flatline kills momentum** - only use for special moments
- **Implied tension is sophisticated** - notice the absence, don't just drop energy
- **Listener attention follows the human voice** - use vocals strategically
- **Not all techniques are equally powerful** - arrangement moves mountains, lyrics whisper

The goal: Create a song where listeners feel pulled along your energy curve without consciously thinking about it.

Based on "The Addiction Formula" by Friedemann Findeisen (2015).
